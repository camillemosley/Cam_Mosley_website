Understanding how climate change and angler interactions shape freshwater fish biology,

I am a fisheries ecologist investigating how angler dynamics, climate change, and other global change factors impact freshwater fish biology and ecology across scales. My work integrates field surveys, whole-lake experiments, creel surveys, and quantitative modeling to inform conservation and fisheries management. I am currently an Research Scientist in Dr. Gretchen Hansen's lab at the University of Minnesota.

    Research

    Mosley group research aims to combine aquaitic biology, fisheries ecology,and limnology to investigate fish population responses to ecosystem change

    Food Web Ecology

    Ecological Niche Breadth and Overlap in Lake Fish

    In this project I use stable isotope data collected from mutiple fish species across over 20 lakes in Minnesota to investigate global patterns in ecological niche theory. I combine climate, lake characteristics, and fish physiology data to determine the relative importance of cross scale drivers on niche breadth and overlap of freshwater fish populations. I used bayseian mixing models to compute ecological niche metrics and random forest model to understand predictor variables

    Fisheries Technology

    Forward Facing Sonar Impacts on MN angler catch rates

    In this project, our goal is to understand how forward-facing sonar usage impacts Minnesota fisheries. We will assess the current prevalence of angler technology used by Minnesotans for several sport fish species using creel survey data from multiple MN counties. Then we will quantify the impact of forward-facing sonar vs 2D sonar on angler catch rates and fish size selectivity of Musky and Walleye populations throughout the year (open and hard water seasons) by experimental angling. Experimental results will allow us to estimate the impact of this technology on future fish populations by modeling hooking mortality rates across variable fish technology and fish abundance scenarios

    Catch and Release Impacts on Fish Behavoir

    Angler Effort Impacts on Recapture Probability and Hyperdepletion

    In this project , I use two small experimental lakes , Peter and Paul Lakes, to determine if hyperdepletion of catch rates occur at increased angling effort levels. I will measure angler catch rates in both lakes and manipulate angling effort in the treatment basin. If results show a decrease in catch rates at increased effort, I will estimate vulnerability return time (rate a fish becomes catchable again) and determine if hyperdepletion may occur at lower angling effort. I predict that using angler effort data from the region will display that hyperdepletion is likely rare within the northern Wisconsin recreational fishery.

    Sport Fish Angler Catch Rates

    Influence of Species and Habiat on Hyperstability of Catch Rates

    In this project, I use a multiple-model comparison approach to evaluate whether the magnitude of catch rate hyperstability differs among six popular freshwater sport fishes or across lakes that vary in habitat availability. I leverage long-term data describing fish population abundances and angler catch rates provided by the Wisconsin Department of Natural Resources (WDNR) to test my hypotheses that hyperstability is stronger for species that aggregate within lakes.
